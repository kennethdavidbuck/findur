# Citation verification

Verified against the cited first-party pages on 2026-09-19. Verdicts assess support for the report's material uses of each reference, not the product recommendation itself.

| Ref | Verdict | Concise evidence / required confidence change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [1] | **Supported** | The OAuth guide states that an OAuth app needs a Commercial account; consent comes from Personal users; `openid`, `email`, `read`, and `webhook` have the stated roles; the flow is authorization-code with PKCE; the client is confidential and production redirects require HTTPS. It also confirms the five-user test limit, production KYC, token storage guidance, existing Personal connections, and no separate OAuth broker approval. No downgrade. |

It contrasts OAuth bearer tokens and Personal-managed connections with that model. No downgrade. |
| [3] | **Supported** | The account-data guide documents account details, positions/holdings, balances, orders, and activities; it states that orders are intraday with more granular timestamps and limited lookback, while activities update daily and can lack transaction time. The proposed derived matching features are expressly an inference in the report, rather than a claimed documented capability. No downgrade. |
| [4] | **Supported** | The syncing guide says transactions are daily and one day delayed, identifies plan-dependent holdings behavior, and states `ACCOUNT_HOLDINGS_UPDATED` is sent even if holdings did not change. The webhook guide documents connection/account lifecycle event types. This supports the eventual-consistency and non-diff interpretation. No downgrade. |
| [5] | **Partly supported** | The compliance policy requires clear disclosure of data use/sharing, limits *market data* to its licensed user, and makes signals/portfolio analysis pull-based. It does **not** itself say that all brokerage/account data is restricted to the originating user, nor does it explicitly address cross-user derived compatibility attributes. Downgrade the report's **high** confidence that public materials resolve this as an unapproved launch gate to **medium**: the policy establishes a real ambiguity and need for written confirmation, but does not expressly prohibit the proposed derived-data flow. |
| [6] | **Supported** | The developer terms prohibit selling/licensing data obtained from SnapTrade; prohibit mining, reselling, or repackaging end-user data to third parties without express written consent of both the end user and SnapTrade; and prohibit storing, reselling, or redistributing market data. They also require security controls and deletion on request. No additional downgrade; the terms still do not expressly classify a coarse derived compatibility result, which is properly treated as an ambiguity. |
