# Market Landscape: Findur

## Verdict

There is a credible, differentiated opening for a **portfolio-informed compatibility** experience, but it is a high-trust dating product rather than an investment-data feature. The closest public comparable found, Score, frames the category around financial responsibility and originally used a credit-score entry threshold; premium dating services such as The League frame exclusivity around ambition and curated access. Findur can be meaningfully distinct if connected portfolio data changes private matching and carefully consented disclosure—not if it becomes a public wealth leaderboard or a decorative verification badge.

## What exists and how it is positioned

- **Score is the nearest direct comparable.** Its February 2026 relaunch says it is a dating app for financial responsibility and relationship compatibility; its original 2024 product used a minimum-credit-score participation requirement. That validates interest in money as a dating signal, while leaving room for Findur's investment-style, consent-led, non-score positioning. Treat Score's product claims as company-reported until independently tested. [Score press release](https://scoredating.app/press-release)
- **Premium/ambition dating is an adjacent substitute, not the same product.** The League sells curated, concierge-style dating for “ambitious” members, including waitlist-skipping membership and an exclusive matchmaking program. Findur should avoid copying status-gating; its defensible proposition is voluntary evidence and compatibility controls. [The League membership](https://www.theleague.com/membership/) · [The League matchmaking](https://www.theleague.com/matchmaking/)
- **The mainstream market remains large and competitive.** Match Group reports 13.3M payers, $3.5B LTM revenue, and Tinder as the most-downloaded dating app worldwide (as of Q2 2026). A new dating app therefore needs a materially different experience—not just another filter—also consistent with Apple’s rule that new dating apps must be meaningfully different or improved. [Match Group IR](https://ir.matchgroup.com/) · [Apple App Review Guidelines 4.3(b)](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/uk/)

## Trends and constraints that matter to the PRD

- **Interest is in financial values, not merely wealth.** Consumer research and the close comparable both describe financial compatibility through habits, responsibility, goals, risk and communication. Portfolio value alone is an incomplete and potentially misleading proxy. Product language and matching explanations should therefore emphasize opt-in derived traits (e.g., diversification preference or activity recency), never a universal desirability score. [Experian’s current dating guidance](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/questions-to-ask-your-date-about-money/) · [Candid’s five-dimension framing](https://candid.partners/)
- **Romance and investment fraud are directly coupled.** The FTC describes romance scammers moving conversations off-platform and using stock/crypto investment pitches or requests for money. Portfolio visibility can increase the perceived payoff for targeting, so safety cannot be deferred to chat launch. [FTC consumer alert](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/02/looking-love-watch-out-scammers) · [FTC romance-scam hub](https://consumer.ftc.gov/romance-scams)
- **Mobile distribution imposes baseline safety operations.** Apple requires UGC/social apps to filter objectionable material, offer reporting and blocking, publish contact information, and respond promptly; Google Play requires ongoing moderation plus report/block features and requires core dating apps to block minors. [Apple UGC guideline 1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/uk/) · [Google Play UGC policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937?hl=en-GB) · [Google Play age-restricted functionality](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16302250?hl=en-GB)
- **Connected brokerage data is broad, mutable, and not a net-worth statement.** SnapTrade exposes account value, holdings, cash/buying power, orders and activities; orders may be intraday while activities are daily, and a disabled connection stops live data until the user reauthorizes. OAuth sharing is currently read-only, and SnapTrade says the app receives a scoped token rather than Personal API credentials. [SnapTrade account data](https://docs.snaptrade.com/docs/account-data) · [connections and reauthorization](https://docs.snaptrade.com/docs/connections) · [OAuth model](https://docs.snaptrade.com/docs/getting-started)
- **The regulatory direction reinforces data minimization, though implementation timing is unsettled.** CFPB’s current 1033 page says its compliance dates were stayed in October 2025. Its current third-party obligation text nevertheless provides a useful design bar: collect/use/retain only what is reasonably necessary for the requested service, do not use the data for targeted advertising, cross-selling, or sale, and limit collection duration. This is not a conclusion that Findur is currently subject to every 1033 provision; counsel should determine scope by launch model and jurisdiction. [CFPB status](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/other-applicable-requirements/personal-financial-data-rights/) · [12 CFR 1033.421](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1033/421/)

## PRD implications

1. **Make differentiation testable:** candidate eligibility/ranking must use portfolio-derived inputs, while the card displays only standardized, user-approved bands or traits. No raw holdings, exact value, account identifiers, full performance, or claim of overall financial health by default.
2. **Define consent as a lifecycle:** show the precise data categories and purpose before connect; separate private matching inputs from visible disclosure; show source coverage and last-refresh time; support disconnect, revocation, deletion/retention handling, and an explicit degraded/reauthorization state.
3. **Ship abuse controls with discovery, not only chat:** block, report, review queue/escalation path, anti-harassment rules, age gate, and clear warnings/prohibitions for money requests, investment solicitation, and off-platform coercion. Suppress financial disclosure to blocked/reported users immediately.
4. **Do not rank people by wealth or sell access to them:** disclosure tiers should govern what may be seen and who may initiate, with recipient protection and an explanation of why a candidate appears. Avoid labels such as “verified wealthy,” scores of financial worthiness, or monetization tied to access to a person’s financial data.
5. **Keep the first milestone deliberately narrow:** test OAuth/read-only connection, one private test portfolio, synthetic candidates, freshness/disconnect states, and comprehension of the above boundaries. Production rollout needs a separate security, privacy, moderation, identity/age-assurance, and legal review gate.

## Evidence limits and refresh triggers

This scan found one close publicly described competitor (Score), several adjacent premium/intentional-dating substitutes, and no independently verified, scaled app that publicly documents SnapTrade-style brokerage connections as its primary discovery mechanism. The category gap is therefore evidence of differentiation, not proof of demand. Refresh before public launch: Score’s live product and pricing, SnapTrade’s OAuth/commercial terms and broker coverage, app-store rules, and jurisdiction-specific privacy/consumer-protection advice.
