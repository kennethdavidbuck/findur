# Accessibility + Trust Review — Findur

## Overall verdict

The spine pair is unusually complete for this stage: it establishes WCAG 2.2 AA as a real cross-theme/cross-locale contract, preserves non-visual and non-gesture paths through Discovery, and treats OAuth access, account inclusion, private derivation, owner-only data, disclosure, and synthetic candidates as distinct concepts. It is **not yet safe to treat as an implementation-ready contract**, however. Five high-severity gaps sit exactly at consent-reducing or permission-invalidating transitions, where the current text permits inaccessible focus loss or continued use/disclosure after the owner has expressed a more private choice.

The right response is a targeted spine update, not a larger production-safety program. Reporting/moderation operations, identity assurance, and real-user cross-user financial sharing remain correctly outside the owner-only synthetic milestone.

## Accessibility verdict — adequate, close to strong

The contract covers the load-bearing WCAG surface well: native semantics, keyboard operation, visible focus, screen-reader state updates, explicit non-swipe actions, chart summaries/tables, hidden-photo semantics, non-color encoding, zoom/reflow, reduced motion, EN/FR language behavior, theme parity, responsive equivalence, and standalone/deep-link recovery are all present. The following gaps materially affect the core flows.

### Findings

1. **High — Account selection lacks a complete accessible selection-and-progress contract.** `Account Inclusion Control` requires none selected by default, individual selection, Select all, a coverage count, saving, recalculation, and failure, but the spine does not define checkbox-group semantics, the mixed state of Select all, or announcements as the count and committed set change (`EXPERIENCE.md:95`, `EXPERIENCE.md:120`, `EXPERIENCE.md:145-147`, `EXPERIENCE.md:176-178`, `EXPERIENCE.md:318-325`; `DESIGN.md:425`). A screen-reader or keyboard user could be unable to tell whether Select all selected every usable account, whether the displayed count is a draft or committed scope, or when Discovery became available. *Fix:* Specify a named checkbox group; give Select all `checked` / `mixed` / `unchecked` semantics; keep unavailable accounts unselectable with an associated reason; expose draft and committed coverage separately; and announce saving, recalculating, success, and failure once without moving focus.

2. **High — Privacy invalidation removes focused content without a deterministic focus destination.** Disclosure downgrade, account exclusion, disconnect, freshness/safety invalidation, and revalidation may immediately remove the active Candidate Detail or close sensitive views, while the accessibility floor only says focus is restored “logically” and explicitly defines restoration for opening/closing Candidate Detail (`EXPERIENCE.md:61`, `EXPERIENCE.md:102-108`, `EXPERIENCE.md:123`, `EXPERIENCE.md:146-147`, `EXPERIENCE.md:169`, `EXPERIENCE.md:192`, `EXPERIENCE.md:202`, `EXPERIENCE.md:347-358`). If the focused node disappears, assistive technology can land at the document root or retain a stale virtual position. *Fix:* For every invalidation transition, move focus to the replacement `Recovery Panel` heading or a dedicated status heading, announce why the content was removed, and define the next safe control. When a confirmation is cancelled, restore focus to its invoker; when a route closes, move focus to the destination heading, not to a removed card.

3. **Medium — Disclosure-level changes do not have committed control and preview semantics.** The three levels alter a large preview and privacy boundary, but the spine only supplies general “radio-like” roving focus guidance and screen-reader change announcements (`EXPERIENCE.md:96`, `EXPERIENCE.md:105`, `EXPERIENCE.md:136`, `EXPERIENCE.md:147`, `EXPERIENCE.md:174-192`, `EXPERIENCE.md:252-259`; `DESIGN.md:426`). An implementation could expose three styled buttons and silently replace a dense preview, making it difficult to distinguish inspected, selected, and saved levels. *Fix:* Define the control as one labelled native radio group (or equivalent single-select semantics); keep preview-only inspection separate from the saved value; associate each option with a concise hidden/bucketed/derived/exact description; and announce only a short preview summary plus saved/not-saved status.

4. **Medium — Post-match Photo semantics stop at announcing the reveal.** The pre-match state correctly suppresses hidden alt text and announces “Photo hidden until Mutual Match,” and the Mutual Match is announced once, but the revealed image's accessible name/description is not defined (`EXPERIENCE.md:92`, `EXPERIENCE.md:104`, `EXPERIENCE.md:145`, `EXPERIENCE.md:149`, `EXPERIENCE.md:275-280`, `EXPERIENCE.md:351-355`; `DESIGN.md:422`, `DESIGN.md:434`). A non-visual user can learn that a reveal occurred without receiving the matched identity image's intended equivalent. *Fix:* On reveal, replace the obscure object with an actual image whose concise alt text identifies it as the candidate's Photo (for example, “Photo of Alex”) or mark it decorative only if adjacent identity text is explicitly declared equivalent; never infer appearance traits into alt text.

5. **Medium — Two border tokens contradict the stated 3:1 non-text contrast contract when used as load-bearing boundaries.** `{colors.border}` is approximately 2.05:1 against the light surface, and `{colors.border-dark}` is approximately 2.31:1 against the dark surface. Yet those tokens are assigned to navigation, Candidate Card, and Chart Data Table boundaries while the prose promises at least 3:1 for controls and meaningful graphical objects (`DESIGN.md:22`, `DESIGN.md:51`, `DESIGN.md:160-182`, `DESIGN.md:202-208`, `DESIGN.md:380`, `DESIGN.md:416-424`). This is not a blanket failure—`border-strong` pairs pass—but consumers cannot tell when the weak border is decorative versus required to perceive a component. *Fix:* Reserve `border` / `border-dark` for nonessential separators, use `border-strong` / `border-strong-dark` for any required component boundary or chart mark, and change the Candidate Card dark border and any essential table/navigation boundary accordingly. Record the verified pairing matrix rather than a blanket token claim.

6. **Medium — OAuth results, gates, and cross-route recovery lack explicit initial-focus rules.** The spine defines safe route/session resolution and named OAuth/gate outcomes, but only Candidate Detail has a precise focus-on-entry target (`EXPERIENCE.md:55-61`, `EXPERIENCE.md:118-123`, `EXPERIENCE.md:146-147`, `EXPERIENCE.md:163-170`, `EXPERIENCE.md:327-336`). After a provider redirect, expired session, or deep-link gate, a keyboard or screen-reader user could arrive with focus at browser chrome or on an unrelated shell control and miss the result. *Fix:* On every full route transition, focus the route/result heading after the title is updated; on an in-place gate replacement, focus the gate heading; preserve the originating control only for same-route retry/cancel. Announce the outcome and recovery action once, without duplicating the heading through a live region.

## Trust, consent, privacy, and safety verdict — adequate, but unsafe at privacy-decreasing transitions

The overall model is sound. The spines explicitly preserve default-none inclusion, Select all, Discovery gating, source coverage and freshness, local/provider disconnect boundaries, cache/history invalidation, neutral metadata and launch surfaces, live-owner versus Synthetic Candidate provenance, anti-solicitation/non-advisory language, screenshot limits, and account-granular provider uncertainty. The defects below are narrower but important because they determine what happens after a person chooses less use or less disclosure.

### Findings

1. **High — Pre-OAuth consent and post-OAuth account inclusion form an ambiguous two-stage consent sequence.** `Consent Panel` says explicit consent precedes OAuth and derivation, while the flow asks for private matching use before the owner knows which accounts OAuth will return; only afterward does default-none inclusion determine which accounts Findur may use (`EXPERIENCE.md:107`, `EXPERIENCE.md:174-180`, `EXPERIENCE.md:240-243`, `EXPERIENCE.md:318-323`). The three-layer separation is stated, but the action at each stage is not: an implementation could treat the first continue as blanket matching consent or begin fetching/deriving before inclusion confirmation. *Fix:* Define staged consent explicitly: pre-OAuth continue authorizes the named provider access/categories and retrieval of the minimum account metadata needed for selection; no portfolio derivation or Discovery use begins; post-OAuth confirmation authorizes Findur use only for the selected accounts and then starts source retrieval/derivation. Copy must name both stages and avoid saying “connected” means “included.”

2. **High — Account-exclusion failure handling contradicts immediate purge requirements.** The state and lifecycle rules say excluded-account source, derived, cache, preview, and revalidation output disappears before completion is confirmed and never returns, but Flow 8 says a save/recalculation failure retains the prior committed inclusion set (`EXPERIENCE.md:120`, `EXPERIENCE.md:178`, `EXPERIENCE.md:202`, `EXPERIENCE.md:322-325`). For an exclusion, retaining the old set means continuing to use the very account the owner just confirmed should no longer be used. *Fix:* Split additive and subtractive failures. On confirmed exclusion, immediately suspend the account from all use/output, invalidate active/prefetched/history/offline state, and keep Discovery gated while purge/recalculation retries; never silently roll back to the broader set. A failed addition may retain the narrower prior committed set. State exactly when local deletion is complete versus recalculation pending.

3. **High — Disclosure-downgrade failure handling permits the same fail-open interpretation.** The contract says a downgrade immediately removes no-longer-permitted active, prefetched, cached, previewed, and history-restored detail, but the Flow 2 failure rule merely retains choices and identifies what was not applied (`EXPERIENCE.md:61`, `EXPERIENCE.md:108`, `EXPERIENCE.md:192`, `EXPERIENCE.md:202`, `EXPERIENCE.md:252-259`). A consumer could keep the previously higher disclosure active until a retry succeeds. *Fix:* Treat a confirmed downgrade as fail-closed: immediately suppress higher-level output and block any new rendering at that level; persist/retry the narrower policy in the background; never restore the broader level through save failure, Back, cache, or offline state. Only an explicit, separately confirmed re-upgrade may broaden visibility.

4. **Medium — The Source-to-Experience Trace collapses permission boundaries it is supposed to teach.** The visual component is only a three-stage source → derived → visible path, while the experience contract requires separate understanding of OAuth access, included-account inputs, private matching derivation, owner-only exact data, and candidate-visible disclosure (`DESIGN.md:311-319`, `DESIGN.md:436`; `EXPERIENCE.md:105-107`, `EXPERIENCE.md:172-190`, `EXPERIENCE.md:245-246`). A reviewer can leave believing that every derived input is visible, or that visible Full Detail is the same as owner-only live data. *Fix:* Make the trace branch after included source data into at least three labelled outputs: owner-only exact view, private matching input, and candidate-visible disclosure. Show blocked/unsupported stages and Disclosure Level effects on the candidate-visible branch only; carry live-owner versus synthetic provenance through every branch.

5. **Medium — “Purge all locally held source data” conflicts with the need to keep excluded connected accounts discoverable and re-selectable.** Account settings must list all connected accounts with provider/account labels even when none are included, but exclusion is described as purging all locally held source data (`EXPERIENCE.md:95`, `EXPERIENCE.md:120`, `EXPERIENCE.md:176-180`; `DESIGN.md:425`). Without a boundary, one implementation may retain financial payloads unnecessarily while another may remove the account identity needed to explain provider access and permit re-inclusion. *Fix:* Define the minimal retained connection inventory separately from financial source data: retain only the least provider/account metadata needed to show authorization and selection status (masked where appropriate), with no values, holdings, activities, or derived signals; fetch or reauthorize on explicit re-inclusion as architecture requires. Reflect this distinction in disconnect and exclusion copy.

6. **Medium — Screenshot and inference limits appear after exposure rather than before a risky disclosure choice.** The only explicit acknowledgment that screenshots, memory, and inference cannot be revoked is attached to downgrade behavior (`EXPERIENCE.md:192`), although Snapshot/Holdings/Full Detail selection and preview are where the owner decides whether exact values and transaction detail may become visible (`EXPERIENCE.md:96`, `EXPERIENCE.md:174-190`, `EXPERIENCE.md:252-259`). A later warning cannot support informed consent to Full Detail. *Fix:* Put a concise persistence/inference limitation in the Disclosure Level preview before save, with stronger proximity for Full Detail, and repeat it in the pre-connection limitations summary without turning it into a legal wall. Keep the downgrade acknowledgment as a reminder, not the first disclosure.

7. **Medium — Inclusion expansion does not require the same explicit scope confirmation as removal.** `Account Inclusion Control` requires consequence acknowledgment when removing accounts, but later adding or re-including an account expands private matching inputs and retained source data without an explicit confirmation rule (`EXPERIENCE.md:95`, `EXPERIENCE.md:176-180`, `EXPERIENCE.md:318-325`). A checkbox change could therefore start derivation immediately even though initial selection uses a confirm step. *Fix:* Treat edits as a draft set. Before applying additions, summarize the newly included account(s), data categories, private purpose, coverage change, and whether provider access is broader than Findur inclusion; begin retrieval/derivation only after explicit Apply/Confirm. Do not require a modal for every checkbox—one scoped confirmation for the draft is sufficient.

## Cross-cutting mechanical notes

- All six frontmatter sources resolve, and the selected Constellation artifact is correctly declared subordinate to the spines. The direction artifact's span-based “tabs,” prototype-sized text, and low-contrast decorative lines must not be copied as component semantics.
- Component names are aligned across the two spines. Freshness vocabulary is not: DESIGN frontmatter offers `current`, `pending`, `stale`, and `unusable`, while the component/experience contract names connected, syncing, stale, needs reauthorization, failed, and disconnected (`DESIGN.md:223-231`, `DESIGN.md:424`; `EXPERIENCE.md:94`, `EXPERIENCE.md:121`, `EXPERIENCE.md:194-202`). Add an explicit state-to-token mapping so consumers do not invent misleading labels or colors.
- The open account-granular provider-authorization question is correctly preserved as an architecture/provider blocker (`EXPERIENCE.md:178`, `EXPERIENCE.md:363`). Acceptance must verify the user-facing distinction even if SnapTrade authorization is provider-wide.
- Installed/standalone, service-worker, social-preview, history, and task-switcher protections are appropriately handed to architecture/build while remaining UX acceptance constraints (`DESIGN.md:367`, `EXPERIENCE.md:24`, `EXPERIENCE.md:127`, `EXPERIENCE.md:204-214`, `EXPERIENCE.md:369`). Platform support must be verified rather than inferred from generic PWA behavior.
- The owner-only/synthetic milestone boundary is consistently preserved. The market-research call for production block/report/moderation controls does not create a current milestone defect because the final PRD expressly prohibits public multi-user interaction and chat.
